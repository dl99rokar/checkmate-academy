Start with the fundamentals

Strong chess comes from repeating a few good habits. Learn one idea, then look for it in your next game.

1. Control the center

The four central squares help your pieces reach both sides of the board. Try to influence them with pawns and pieces.

2. Develop your pieces

Bring knights and bishops into the game early. Avoid moving the same piece repeatedly without a clear reason.

3. Keep your king safe

Castling usually gets your king away from the center and brings a rook toward the middle.

4. Check before every move

Ask: Can my opponent check me, capture something, or create a direct threat?

5. Know your pieces

Queen moves in every straight or diagonal direction. Rooks move straight, bishops diagonally, knights in an L-shape, and kings one square.

6. Win by checkmate

The goal is to attack the enemy king so it is in check and has no legal way to escape. Capturing the king is not a chess move.

Before you move — a simple checklist

1. What is my opponent threatening?   2. Is any of my piece undefended?   3. Can I improve a piece or win material safely?   4. Is my king safe?
